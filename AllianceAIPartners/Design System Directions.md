## How the scores work

Each direction gets five scores from 1 to 10:

- Project fit: how well it fits the project details in the Brand DNA: an AI partner for small and mid-sized businesses abroad, our three goals and 70-plus services, honest pricing, plain English, and people reviewing the AI's work.
- Company goals: how directly it serves our three goals (scale AI, solve problems with AI, and grow) and the business goal of winning the trust of new buyers.
- Connection: how strongly it expresses partnership: the meaning of "alliance" (binding together), one team with one goal, and the relationship with each partner.
- Uniqueness: how distinct it looks next to AI agencies, AI labs, and offshore staffing firms, with low risk of clichés or of looking like a known brand.
- Personality: how well it matches our personality (the Sage and the Ruler: a calm, plain-spoken, honest expert, something like an architect), and how personal and ownable it feels.

The overall score is the average of the five. Ties go to the higher connection score, then the higher uniqueness score. The scores are judgment calls, informed by the research below.

Logo and website strength are rated too, for reference only. They aren't part of the overall score, but they show which directions would give the strongest logo and the strongest website system.

## What the research found

Research on 23 and 24 October 2026 looked at 15 AI agencies that sell to small businesses in the US, the UK, and Australia, at the big AI labs, at trend reports, and at existing logos and names our ideas might resemble.

How AI agencies look:

- Nine of the 15 use a dark background with a glow or gradient, such as [Morningside](https://www.morningside.ai/), [Aristral](https://aristral.com/), and [Autosyntech](https://www.autosyntech.com/).
- Several use the default software look: slate text with an indigo accent (#4F46E5), like [OptiWork](https://optiwork.ai/) and [AI Elevation](https://aielevation.co.uk/).
- A warm-paper look is spreading: an off-white background, a serif headline, mono labels, and one hot accent. [AutomateNexus](https://automatenexus.com/) uses paper #F2F1EC and ink #16171C, and [FlowWorks](https://flowworks.com.au/) uses cream with vermilion.
- Orange is the most common accent (five sites), then gold (four).
- None of the 15 uses a Swiss grid, a transit map, signage, or a publishing look, and only [13Labs](https://www.13labs.au/) is brutalist. A second spot check found none using any of the Draft 3 themes either: levels, documents, theater, architecture, metals, typewriters, or proofreading.
- Our promises are crowded too. AI Elevation promises plain English and a fixed price, and AutomateNexus puts its prices in its hero. The design has to carry the partnership story, not just the price.
- Offshore staffing firms repeat their own look: blue or navy with an orange button, grids of staff headshots, world maps, and claims like "Top 1%" ([Penbrothers](https://penbrothers.com), [MicroSourcing](https://www.microsourcing.com), [Emapta](https://www.emapta.com)).

How the big AI labs look:

- Anthropic's palette is near-black #141413 and ivory #FAF9F5 with a clay orange #D97757, and its icon is a heavy black A ([Anthropic brand guidelines](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/brand-guidelines/SKILL.md)).
- Mistral uses cream with orange, yellow, and red, and a pixel-art M ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/brand)). OpenAI is black and white ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/brand/)), and Rabbit's AI device is bright orange on warm gray ([Rabbit](https://www.rabbit.tech/)).
- So warm off-white with orange now reads as "AI lab."

What trend sources say:

- In a 2024 test with 107 people, nobody linked the sparkles icon with AI unless it had a label ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-sparkles-icon-problem/)).
- AI logos are converging on round, spiral marks ([Creative Bloq, 2025](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/why-do-all-ai-company-logos-look-the-same)), and purple is the most common color for AI features, likely because of software defaults ([Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/color)).
- B2B brands are moving to real people, plain naming, and expressive type ([Bop Design, 2026](https://www.bopdesign.com/bop-blog/b2b-branding-trends-for-2026-and-2027/)).
- When everything looks the same, buyers compare on price ([Creative Bloq, 2026](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/everything-looks-the-same-now-what)). For a firm that's already cheaper, a distinct look is what keeps the conversation about results, which is the Brand DNA's rule that price comes second.

## Rules every direction follows

- Text passes WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 for body text, and 3:1 for large text (24 pixels, or 19 pixels bold), buttons, icons, and graphics. Every ratio in this note was calculated, more than 400 pairs across the drafts.
- Light is the default. Buyers read in daytime offices, and proposals get printed. Each direction also lists a dark version for slides, video, and product screens.
- Color never works alone. Status messages, chart series, and line colors always carry an icon, a label, or a word.
- No AI clichés: sparkles, glowing edges or orbs, purple gradients, robots, brains, circuit boards, a human hand touching a robot hand, or mascots.
- No offshore-staffing clichés: navy with an orange button, grids of smiling headshots, world maps with pins, or "Top 1%" badges.
- None of the three stock looks: cream with a serif and terracotta, near-black with neon, or newspaper hairlines with an italic serif and tiny mono.
- No overused display fonts, such as Inter, Roboto, Montserrat, Poppins, Space Grotesk, Fraunces, Playfair Display, DM Sans, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans, or Geist.
- Borrow principles, never marks: no Penguin bands or penguin, no London roundel or Johnston type, no Braun raised A, no Munich 1972 pictograms, no Olivetti marks, and no Swiss flag.
- Stay clear of the other Alliances: no capital A in a colored square, no triangle A, no black on yellow, no overlapping circles, and no navy bold italic. Climbing themes belong to BELAY.
- Direction names are internal working names and never go on the website. Plain English words are often already company names, several of them in AI.
- Every logo works at 16 pixels and in one color.
- Photos show our real team and real work. No stock photos and no AI-generated people.
- Every font is free for commercial use under the SIL Open Font License. Each was downloaded from Google Fonts on 23 October 2026 and checked for figures that line up in tables. The three without them (Cabin, Libre Caslon Display, and League Spartan) set headlines only.

## Foundations every direction shares

These stay the same whichever direction wins, so this part of the system can be built now:

- Grid: 12 columns, content up to 1,200 pixels wide, with 24-pixel gutters on desktop and 16 pixels on phones.
- Spacing on a 4-pixel base: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, and 128.
- Type scale, desktop then phone: display 60 and 40, heading 1 48 and 34, heading 2 36 and 28, heading 3 28 and 24, heading 4 22 and 20, large body 19, body 17, small 15, and caption 13. Line height is 1.1 for display, 1.2 for headings, and 1.5 for body, with lines up to about 70 characters.
- Numbers: prices and tables use tabular figures, right-aligned, always with the currency. In CSS, set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on price cells, because most fonts only line up their figures when asked to.
- Status colors, each with an icon and a word: success #276B43, warning #8A5A00, and error #B3261E. Check them against every new palette's page, card, and panel colors, at 4.5:1 or better.
- Focus: a 2-pixel ring in the direction's ink, 2 pixels outside the element.
- Touch targets: at least 44 by 44 pixels.
- Motion: 150 to 300 ms with ease-out, nothing that loops, and no motion for people who turn it off.
- Logo use: clear space equal to the wordmark's cap height on every side, the symbol at least 16 pixels, and the wordmark at least 96 pixels wide.

## How to combine colors

1. Use a 60-30-10 split: about 60% background, 30% text and secondary neutrals, and about 10% accent. Bold directions go up to 20% accent. Each palette's share column should follow this.
2. One accent, one job: the main action and the one thing to notice. A direction with several colors uses them to sort things, never as decoration.
3. Light accents carry dark text, and dark accents carry white text. Each palette says which.
4. Keep a neutral between two accents. Two strong colors touching at full strength fight each other.
5. For dark mode, swap the background and the ink, then use a brighter version of the accent.
6. Don't separate meaning by red and green alone, because many people can't tell them apart.
7. In print, convert to CMYK and match colors with the printer. The hex values here are the reference.

## Sources and method

The research for Draft 3 ran on 23 and 24 October 2026. It covered the design history behind each style, 15 AI agencies in the US, the UK, and Australia plus a second spot check, the big AI labs, trend reports, logo and name clashes for every direction, and companies named Alliance.
