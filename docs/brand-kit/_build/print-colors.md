Plainly: **Alliance Blue #1B56FF is outside the CMYK gamut and outside every Pantone ink** (nearest ink dE 5.5, CMYK dE 8.0 to 8.4). **Ribbon Cyan #00D5FF is partly outside CMYK** (dE 4.2 to 5.5) and its nearest ink is dE 4.1 away. dE2000 reading guide: under 1 is invisible side by side, 1 to 2 is a close match, 2 to 4 is noticeable side by side, above 5 looks like a different colour.

## CMYK builds

ICC conversions with Pillow ImageCms (littleCMS 2.18): sRGB to each profile, relative colorimetric with black point compensation. **raw** is the 8-bit result, **clean** is whole percent with 1-2 % traces dropped and 98-99 % made solid, each only when it costs at most 0.5 dE2000. **rt dE** is the round trip back to Lab with the same intent and BPC, which isolates gamut loss. **paper dE** reads the build back without BPC, which is what the printed sheet measures against its own white: dark colours lose depth there because a press black is lighter than screen black. The Lab readback is averaged over neighbouring 8-bit inputs (precision about 0.05 dE).

### Document profile

**Recommended: Coated GRACoL 2006 (ISO 12647-2:2004)** (`CoatedGRACoL2006.icc`). Darker black point and higher ink limit (TAC 329 against 295 on SWOP v2): the navy prints at paper-relative dE 5.5 on GRACoL 2006 against 8.6 on SWOP v2, the blue matches as well, and GRACoL 2006 / FOGRA39 are the ISO 12647-2 coated-sheetfed conditions most brand collateral is printed to, while SWOP v2 is a web-offset publication condition. SWOP v2 is better only for Ribbon Cyan (dE 4.2 against 5.5). Coated FOGRA39 is the equivalent choice for vendors who print to ISO 12647-2 / FOGRA39 (common outside North America); builds for all three are given. Ask each vendor for its printing condition first.

The default build per colour for the CMYK master is the **clean** build on that profile (navy: the low-TAC build). The SWOP v2 column of the summary is the drop-in set if the master stays on SWOP.

## Practical notes

**K100 versus rich navy.** The Monochrome Black (1-colour) artboards are `C0 M0 Y0 K100` and nothing else: stamps, embossing and foil dies, laser engraving and 1-colour jobs need a single plate, and a four-colour black would misregister on fine detail. Set K100 type to overprint. Navy is different: large dark-mode backgrounds and big shapes take the multi-ink navy (`C56 M28 Y0 K93` on GRACoL 2006, `C39 M20 Y0 K95` on SWOP v2) because K100 alone prints a flat charcoal (dE about 10 from the navy). Even so, process navy measures about L* 16 on GRACoL and L* 21 on SWOP against L* 9.5 on screen, while the spot ink PANTONE 7547 C sits at L* 10.6, so stationery and anything with fine navy type is best printed with the spot. Keep reversed (white) type out of rich navy at 8 pt bold or larger, and avoid hairlines.

**White ink on dark substrates.** On paper, white is unprinted stock and the Monochrome White artboards are knockouts. On dark, clear or metallic media (vehicle-wrap film, clear decals, dark garments, kraft, dark plastic) white has to be printed: add a spot swatch for the white ink, named exactly as the vendor's RIP expects (Roland VersaWorks, for example, looks for `RDG_WHITE`), give it a visible proxy colour, put it on its own layer set to overprint, and add a white underbase beneath the blue and cyan so they stay bright (choke the underbase slightly so no white halo shows). Wrap printers usually print CMYK on white cast film and convert spots in their RIP: send the Pantone references and ask for a printed swatch. For cut-vinyl signage, pick the nearest stock film colour from the film maker's swatch book using the Pantone references.

**Embroidery.** Thread is matched to colour references, not mixed: give the digitiser the PANTONE C references (2726 C, 305 C, 7547 C) and the hex values, let them map to their thread line (the major thread makers publish Pantone cross-reference charts) and approve a physical sew-out, since glossy rayon or polyester reads brighter than ink. Stitch only the flat lockups (two-colour or 1-colour marks); the 3D gradient cannot be stitched. Keep lettering at least about 6 mm (1/4 in) tall and drop the tagline on small placements. On navy garments use the Monochrome White or the white and cyan mark.

**3D gradient logo with spot inks.** The full-colour gradient master is a process (CMYK) file. If a spot version is needed (for example 2-colour offset, flexo or screen with PANTONE 2726 C and PANTONE 305 C), rebuild the gradient with the spot swatches as stops: Illustrator outputs a DeviceN blend in which each plate carries its own tint ramp. Do not mix process and spot stops in one gradient unless the printer agrees. A tint ramp of one ink only gets lighter, so shading darker than the full-strength ink needs a third, darker ink (a low tint of PANTONE 7547 C overprinting) or the shading flattens. Keep the lightest tint at 3 to 5 % or more (smaller dots drop out and leave a hard edge) and check long gradients for banding. Where two transparent spot inks overlap they print darker than the on-screen blend: check Overprint Preview and Separations Preview and ask for a press proof or drawdown. Screen-printed apparel and embroidery use the flat versions.

**Defining the spot swatches in Illustrator.** Pantone libraries are no longer bundled with Illustrator (Pantone Connect is required), so create each spot by hand: Swatch Options, Color Type = Spot Color, Color Mode = Lab, the Lab D50 values above and the exact name (`PANTONE 2726 C`, `PANTONE 305 C`, `PANTONE 7547 C`; the U names for uncoated jobs). Printers match by the name; the Lab values drive the on-screen preview and any conversion. `illustrator_swatches` in the JSON lists them with the process swatches.

## Method and caveats

- Pantone Lab values are absolute measurements of the guide swatches (D50, 2 deg, white = perfect diffuser) while the brand colours are screen colours (white = paper). Pantone's own conversions compare them the same way; it matters most for the whites, where the coated paper itself (about L* 95) is the closest match.
- The newest Pantone data (Solid Coated V5 in Pantone Connect, with decimals) needs a paid account and was not used. The PCM V4 export equals the V3 export (Aug 2019) for all 1,846 shared colours. PCM V4 and PANTONE+ (2010) differ by mean dE 0.57 (p95 1.20) on the 1314 coated colours they share, and PCM V4 and Pantone's published sRGB by mean dE 0.77 (p95 1.48) for the 1753 inks inside sRGB. Where the value sets disagree on the order (Partner Navy) the difference is inside that spread.
- The measurement condition (M0, M1, M2) shifts Pantone Lab values on paper with optical brighteners, and the colour books used here do not state theirs. Treat dE differences under about 1 as ties.
- pantone.com slab images are lossy WebP, within 1-2 levels of Pantone's sRGB: the pantonecolors.net hex values match them that closely, and PCM V4 Lab sits at mean dE 1.15 (coated) and 1.15 (uncoated) from them. Uncoated sRGB values come only from those slabs.
- littleCMS and Adobe's ACE engine implement black point compensation slightly differently, so Illustrator's own conversion can differ from these builds by 1-2 points per channel. Confirm critical colours with a contract proof.
- The Pastels & Neons guide and 13 colours found only in PANTONE+ 2010 (metallics 871-877 and a few base inks) are kept in `work/print/pantone_ref.json` but excluded from the main ranking.
- `pantone_ref.json` is Pantone colour data taken from third-party mirrors of Pantone's colour books: use it internally for matching and do not publish or ship it with the brand kit.
