## Who we are

Alliance AI Partners is an AI startup that teams up with companies to help them scale AI, solve business problems with AI, and grow. We call these companies our partners, because we work as part of their team.

Our services fall under those three goals:

| Goal | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Scale AI | Strategy and leadership, training and adoption, software and product engineering, running AI for you, robotics and physical AI, talent and teams |
| Solve problems with AI | Automation and AI agents, customer support, knowledge and data, design, security and risk |
| Grow | Marketing, sales and go-to-market, online stores |

The full list of services, including ready-made packages for common industries, is near the end of this guide.

## 1. Purpose: why we exist

> Many companies know AI could help them, but their teams are buried in slow, manual work and don't have the time or the skills to make it happen. We exist to close that gap, so companies can get more done and grow without wearing out their people.

## 2. Vision: where we're going

> By 2035, we want to be a global hub for AI: the partner that companies around the world trust most to help them use AI and grow.

That means:

- Hundreds of partner companies, each starting from what we've learned with the others. Lessons travel across the alliance; partner data never does.
- Long-term relationships, with partners counting on us year after year.
- Our own products, built from problems we've solved many times over.
- A team that the best engineers, designers, and marketers want to join, which gives partners access to great people.

## 3. Mission: what we do every day

> Every day, we work side by side with our partners to find where AI can help, build what they need, keep it running, and help them win more customers.

How we work with a new partner:

1. Understand. We learn how their business works and where it gets stuck.
2. Plan. We pick the right fix. That might be AI or automation, better design, extra developers, or a marketing push. Sometimes it doesn't involve AI at all.
3. Build. Our engineers and designers build it, on our own or inside the partner's team.
4. Run and grow. We keep what we built working, improve it over time, and help the partner find new customers.

## 4. Values

### One team, one goal

When we partner with a company, their goals become our goals. We work as part of their team, and we judge our work by the partner's results, like more sales or hours saved.

- Before we start, we agree on what success looks like, using the partner's own numbers.
- When something goes wrong, we say "we," and we fix it.

### Simple on purpose

A good system stays in the background. People notice that their work got easier and rarely think about what's behind it.

- We pick the simplest solution that does the job.
- Every automation has an owner and a backup plan.
- We write down how everything works, clearly enough for a new hire to follow.

### Trust holds everything up

Partners trust us with their data and the systems their business runs on. We protect that access as if it were our own.

- Partner data stays where the partner decides, and we never use it to train anything they don't own.
- If AI isn't the right answer, we say so, even when it means less work for us.
- We share bad news early, along with a plan to fix it.

### Clear the path

We go first. We try new AI tools on our own business before we bring them to partners, so partners only get what works.

- We use what we sell.
- We adopt a new tool only after it has proven itself in our own work.

## 5. Personality and tone of voice

### Personality

If Alliance AI Partners were a person, they'd be the calm expert in the room. They know AI well, explain it in plain words, and take responsibility for the results.

In brand-strategy terms, our main archetype is the Sage: the wise guide who tells you the truth. Our second archetype is the Ruler: the steady leader who brings order and owns the outcome. Together they make us something like an architect, someone who understands the whole structure and keeps it standing.

We avoid two other archetypes. The Magician promises AI "magic," while business owners want results they can count on. The Creator is mostly about making things, and partners come to us for advice as much as for what we build.

| We are | We are never | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Confident | Arrogant | We give a clear recommendation and explain why. |
| Clear | Hard to follow | We use everyday words and explain any technical term we need. |
| Forward-looking | Overhyped | We talk about where AI is heading when it changes what a partner should do today. |
| Calm | Cold | Under pressure, we slow down and get specific. |
| Loyal | Afraid to push back | We disagree with a partner's plan when we think it will hurt them. |

### Three rules for how we write and speak

#### 1. Start with the result

People pay for outcomes, like hours saved or more customers. Mention the technology only when it helps them make a decision. In writing, open every page and proposal with what changes for the reader. On sales calls, whenever you mention something technical, say what it means for their business.

#### 2. Listen first

On a first sales call, spend the first half asking questions. Repeat the problem back in the partner's own words before suggesting anything, and say so plainly when the answer doesn't involve AI. In writing, show that you understand the reader's situation before offering a fix. Skip the hype and the exclamation marks.

#### 3. Be clear and specific

Use short sentences and everyday words. One real number does more than a string of adjectives. If a competitor could say the same sentence about themselves, rewrite it until they couldn't. Say "we will" instead of "we can," and only publish numbers you can prove.

### Before and after

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "We leverage cutting-edge AI to build seamless automations." | "Your orders now go straight into your system. Nobody types them twice." |
| "Our revolutionary AI will transform your business!" | "Before we suggest anything, walk us through what happens when a new customer request comes in." |
| "We deliver robust, world-class AI solutions." | "Your support assistant answered [1,400] questions last month, and your team handled the [90] that needed a person." |

Numbers in brackets are placeholders. Only publish real figures.

### Words to use and avoid

Use: partner, team, together, build, connect, simple, clear, proven, results, grow.

Avoid: leverage, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, disrupt, supercharge, unleash, unlock, 10x, synergy, magic, ninja, guru, rockstar, hustle, alpha.

Say "partners" for the companies we work with, and keep "customers" for their customers.

Alliance AI Partners becomes that team: we automate slow work, build AI software and support tools, and add developers when you need more hands. ## Notes before launch

1. The service list is long, and a company that seems to do everything can look like a generic agency, which is the image we want to leave behind. On the website and in sales calls, lead with the three goals (scale AI, solve problems, and grow) and let people open the full list from there.
2. Present developer staffing as part of the partnership. "Staffing" can sound like renting out people by the hour. Calling it "embedded developers" makes it clear they work inside the partner's team with all of Alliance behind them.
3. Lead with proof. The full list covers what companies commonly ask for today. Only put a service on the website once we can deliver it well, and show the ones with the most results and case studies first.
4. Pick a starting market. "Many companies" covers almost everyone. Choose one or two kinds of companies to focus on first (the industry table in the full list is a good place to start), do great work there, and let referrals bring in the next ones.
5. Check the name. Many businesses use "Alliance," so run trademark and domain checks before paying for final logo design.
6. Put the data promise in writing. We tell partners their data stays private. Publish a short, plain data policy that backs this up before the promise appears in marketing.
7. Choose the big idea. The logo, colors, fonts, and design system all depend on it, so settle it before any visual work. The options are in [Big Idea Options](Big%20Idea%20Options.md).
